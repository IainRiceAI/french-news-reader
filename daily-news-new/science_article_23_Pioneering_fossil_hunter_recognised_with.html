Pioneering fossil hunter recognised with blue plaque

📅 Mon, 07 Sep 2026 06:01:33 GMT 🔗 BBC Original

Dorothea Bate, née en 1878, avait peu d'éducation formelle mais une fascination pour la faune et la nature.

Dorothea Bate, born in 1878, had little formal education but a fascination with wildlife and nature

A pioneering fossil hunter who was one of the first women to be employed by the Natural History Museum has been recognised with a national blue plaque.

[Error Translate] Dorothea Bate, who died in 1951 aged 72, "transformed our understanding of prehistoric animals and ancient environments" through her research, Historic England said.

Dorothea Bate, who died in 1951 aged 72, "transformed our understanding of prehistoric animals and ancient environments" through her research, Historic England said.

Elle a été reconnue par une plaque au Musée d'Histoire Naturelle, à Tring, Hertfordshire, qui indique dans son inscription que le paléontologue était le "officier en charge de ce musée à partir de 1948".

She has been recognised with a plaque at the Natural History Museum, in Tring, Hertfordshire, which notes in its inscription that the palaeontologist was the "Officer-in-Charge of this museum from 1948".

[Error Translate] Bate was born in Carmarthen, Wales, in 1878 and developed a fascination with the natural world from an early age.

Bate was born in Carmarthen, Wales, in 1878 and developed a fascination with the natural world from an early age.

[Error Translate] She moved with her family to rural Gloucestershire as a young woman and spent hours exploring the surrounding countryside and caves, collecting fossils.

She moved with her family to rural Gloucestershire as a young woman and spent hours exploring the surrounding countryside and caves, collecting fossils.

[Error Translate] She had little formal education, instead largely teaching herself, and in 1900 asked for work behind the scenes at the Natural History Museum in London.

She had little formal education, instead largely teaching herself, and in 1900 asked for work behind the scenes at the Natural History Museum in London.

[Error Translate] Her determination impressed museum staff, earning her a position as an assistant and beginning an association with the institution that would last more than 50 years.

Her determination impressed museum staff, earning her a position as an assistant and beginning an association with the institution that would last more than 50 years.

Elle a publié son premier article scientifique, A Short Account Of A Bone Cave In The Carboniferous Limestone Of The Wye Valley, en 1901.

She published her first scientific paper, A Short Account Of A Bone Cave In The Carboniferous Limestone Of The Wye Valley, in 1901.

Bate entreprit ensuite des expéditions à travers la Méditerranée, voyageant souvent seul dans des grottes isolées à la recherche de preuves d'une vie ancienne.

Bate went on to undertake expeditions across the Mediterranean, often travelling alone to remote caves in search of evidence of ancient life.

À Chypre, elle a découvert une espèce d'éléphant nain jusqu'alors inconnue et, en 1909, aux Baléares, elle a découvert le myotragus, une antilope ressemblant à une chèvre, éteinte.

In Cyprus, she discovered a previously unknown species of dwarf elephant and in 1909, while in the Balearics, she discovered the myotragus - an extinct goat-like antelope.

Elle a publié plus de 80 articles scientifiques, même si elle a passé la majeure partie de sa carrière sans poste rémunéré permanent.

She achieved more than 80 published scientific papers, despite spending most of her career without a permanent paid position.

[Error Translate] In 2017, a blue plaque was dedicated to Bate at Napier House in Carmarthen where she was born.

In 2017, a blue plaque was dedicated to Bate at Napier House in Carmarthen where she was born.

La chasseuse de fossiles, qui fut l'une des premières femmes employées par le Musée d'histoire naturelle, a été récompensée par une plaque bleue nationale.

The fossil hunter, who was one of the first women to be employed by the Natural History Museum, has been recognised with a national blue plaque

Dans une déclaration commune, les co-PDG d'Historic England Claudia Kenyatta et Emma Squire ont déclaré que "la vie de Bate était définie par son intense curiosité et son désir de comprendre le monde naturel".

In a joint statement, co-CEOs of Historic England Claudia Kenyatta and Emma Squire said that Bate's "life was defined by her intense curiosity and desire to understand the natural world".

"Elle a utilisé cette motivation pour donner un sens à la vie ancienne à travers des fragments d'os, lui redonnant vie", ont-ils déclaré.

"She used this drive to make sense of ancient life through fragments of bone, bringing it to life once again," they said.

"Ses recherches ont transformé notre compréhension des animaux préhistoriques et des environnements anciens.

"Her research transformed our understanding of prehistoric animals and ancient environments.

« Non seulement son travail continue d'être inestimable dans le domaine qu'elle a contribué à établir, mais en tant que l'une des premières femmes employées par le Musée d'histoire naturelle, elle a joué un rôle déterminant en ouvrant la voie aux futures femmes scientifiques.

"Not only does her work continue to be invaluable in the field that she helped to establish, but as one of the first women to be employed by the Natural History Museum she was instrumental in paving the way for future women in science.

"Cette plaque garantit que ses contributions pionnières à la science reçoivent la plus large reconnaissance qu'elles méritent."

"This plaque ensures that her pioneering contributions to science receives the wider recognition it deserves."

La ministre du Patrimoine, la baronne Twycross, a déclaré que les réalisations de Bate "ont contribué à briser le plafond de verre et ouvert la voie aux femmes en paléontologie".

Heritage minister Baroness Twycross said Bate's achievements "helped break through the glass ceiling and paved the way for women in palaeontology".

Le Dr Sandra Knapp, directrice de recherche au Musée d'histoire naturelle, a déclaré : « Le rôle des femmes scientifiques dans l'histoire naturelle est souvent caché – mais le travail de Dorothea Bate avec les mammifères fossiles était à la fois innovant et ouvrait la voie à d'autres, malgré les obstacles.

Dr Sandra Knapp, director of research at the Natural History Museum, said: "The role of women scientists in natural history is often hidden - but Dorothea Bate's work with fossil mammals was both innovative and set a path for others, despite obstacles.

"L'installation de cette plaque lui rend non seulement hommage, mais elle rappelle également que les femmes ont été et continuent d'être des contributeurs clés à notre compréhension du monde naturel."

"The installation of this plaque not only honours her but also serves as a reminder that women have been, and continue to be, key contributors to our understanding of the natural world."
