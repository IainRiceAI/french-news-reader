C'était également le cas du personnage de Mitte, Walter 'Flynn' White Jr., fils du personnage de Bryan Cranston dans le drame, récemment nommé meilleur émission de télévision du 21e siècle par le New York Times.

"What I really like is that he was on TV playing a character that had cerebral palsy, but it wasn't about the fact that he had cerebral palsy," says Emma Livingstone, founder and CEO of Up - the Adult Cerebral Palsy Movement.

"When we see RJ Mitte on TV just playing a role, but who also happens to have CP, I think that's really powerful."

Mitte, décédé par suicide lundi à l'âge de 34 ans, n'était pas seulement un acteur - il était également un défenseur et un partisan, travaillant comme ambassadeur pour des organismes de bienfaisance dont United Cerebral Palsy et Can Do Canines, ainsi que la création de la Fondation Mitte, qui offrait des subventions communautaires dans sa ville natale du Texas.

Mitte, who died by suicide on Monday at the age of 34, wasn't just an actor - he was also an advocate and supporter, working as an ambassador for charities including United Cerebral Palsy and Can Do Canines, as well as creating The Mitte Foundation, which offered community grants in his home town in Texas.

Susan Sellers, présidente et chef de la direction de United Cerebral Paralysy, déclare: " Son activité était fondée sur l'engagement de faire en sorte que les personnes atteintes de paralysie cérébrale et d'autres handicaps soient vues, entendues et disposées à atteindre leur plein potentiel. "

Susan Sellers, president and CEO of United Cerebral Palsy, says: "His advocacy was rooted in a commitment to ensuring that people with cerebral palsy and other disabilities are seen, heard, and afforded the opportunity to reach their full potential."

Mitte's legacy, particularly for performers with disabilities, is significant.

Comedian and screenwriter Laurence Clarke concurs. "The more portrayals that we see on TV, that's going to inspire more disabled people to think it's a career for them," he says.

Speaking of Mitte's role in Breaking Bad, Clarke tells the BBC: "What's beautiful about it is that the disability is visibly there, always."

Mitte was aged three when he was diagnosed with cerebral palsy, which the NHS describes as the name for a group of lifelong conditions that affect movement and co-ordination caused by a problem with the brain that develops around birth.

He was cast in Breaking Bad at 14, and told Channel 4 News in 2016 that the show "pioneered a lot of TV and writers to be more brave".

But he added a note of caution, saying: "We still have a long way to go... it's still a business and we have to do that slowly."
