Police investigate donations by Tice company to Reform UK

📅 Sat, 25 Jul 2026 18:04:50 GMT 🔗 BBC Original

Summary: Britain Means Business, owned by the party’s deputy leader Richard Tice, made two donations of £250,000 in the run-up to the 2024 general election.

La police enquête sur les paiements versés à Reform UK par une société contrôlée par le chef adjoint du parti, Richard Tice, a appris BBC News.

Police are investigating payments to Reform UK by a company controlled by the party's deputy leader, Richard Tice, BBC News has learned.

Britain Means Business a fait au parti deux dons de 250 000 £ à l'approche des élections générales de 2024, lorsque Tice était président du parti.

Britain Means Business gave the party two donations of £250,000 in the run-up to the 2024 general election, when Tice was party chairman.

Tice a déclaré que l'argent avait été donné à son entreprise par Fiona Cottrell, une donatrice importante de Reform UK et mère d'un fraudeur condamné ayant des liens étroits avec Nigel Farage.

Tice has said the money was given to his firm by Fiona Cottrell, a significant donor to Reform UK and mother of a convicted fraudster with close links to Nigel Farage.

Tice a déclaré : « C'est la première fois que j'en entends parler. Si c'est vrai, pourquoi la police du Met informe-t-elle la BBC avant de contacter les parties intéressées ? La direction du Met est-elle désormais engagée dans une vaste campagne de diffamation politiquement motivée contre le Parti réformé et ses dirigeants ?

Tice said: "This is the first I have heard of it - if true why are the Met Police briefing the BBC before contacting interested parties? Is the Met leadership now engaged in the widespread politically motivated smear campaign against Reform and its leaders?"

Plus tôt ce mois-ci, le journal externe Times a révélé que deux dons faits directement par Fiona Cottrell à Reform UK en mai 2024 faisaient l'objet d'une enquête de la police métropolitaine.

Earlier this month, the Times newspaper, external revealed that two donations given directly by Fiona Cottrell to Reform UK in May 2024 were being investigated by the Metropolitan Police.

Cette enquête porte sur la législation régissant les élections, qui interdit de dissimuler ou de dissimuler les dons provenant d'un donateur "non autorisé", ou d'utiliser de "fausses" informations sur un don, notamment le montant ou l'identité du donateur.

That investigation relates to legislation governing elections, which bans concealing or disguising donations derived from an "impermissible" donor, or the use of "false" information about a donation including the amount or the identity of the donor.

La BBC a demandé ses commentaires à Fiona Cottrell.

The BBC has asked Fiona Cottrell for comment.

Britain Means Business, qui est uniquement détenu et contrôlé par Tice, a reçu 1 million de livres sterling de Fiona Cottrell en juin 2024.

Britain Means Business, which is solely owned and controlled by Tice, received £1m from Fiona Cottrell in June 2024.

Les informations communiquées à la Commission électorale montrent que la société a ensuite fait don d'un total de 500 000 £ à Reform UK le même mois.

Disclosures to the Electoral Commission show that the company then donated a total of £500,000 to Reform UK in the same month.

Tice a déclaré à la BBC plus tôt ce mois-ci que c'était entièrement son choix que l'entreprise donne la moitié de l'argent de Fiona Cottrell à son parti.

Tice told the BBC earlier this month that it was entirely his choice for the company to give half of the money from Fiona Cottrell to his party.

Les paiements ont été révélés lorsque le journal externe Guardian a révélé qu'ils avaient été signalés à la National Crime Agency dans le cadre de son programme de rapports d'activités suspectes.

The payments came to light when the Guardian newspaper, external revealed they had been flagged to the National Crime Agency as part of its Suspicious Activity Reports programme.

BBC News a appris qu'ils faisaient partie de l'enquête du Met sur les dons à Reform UK.

Now BBC News has learned that they are part of the Met's investigation into donations to Reform UK.

La BBC comprend que le parti n'était pas au courant de l'enquête sur les paiements de Britain Means Business.

The BBC understands that the party was not aware of the investigation into the payments from Britain Means Business.

A Reform UK spokesman said: "Reform takes pride in its stringent vetting of donations and always ensures that the Electoral Commission guidelines on donations, as set out in the Political Parties, Elections and Referendums Act 2000, are strictly followed, as has been the case with donations so far."

All four payments were made in the weeks before the general election on 4 July 2024, when Farage and Tice were first elected to Parliament:

The first direct donation, external from Ms Cottrell of £250,000 appeared in the party's accounts on 9 May

The second, external, also £250,000, appeared a few weeks later on 29 May, just over a month before polling day

One payment, external from Britain Means Business of £250,000 was made on 10 June

Le concept de donateurs « autorisés » et « non autorisés » a été introduit par la loi externe de 2000 sur les partis politiques, les élections et les référendums, qui interdit le financement étranger des partis politiques.

The concept of "permissible" and "impermissible" donors was introduced by the Political Parties, Elections and Referendums Act 2000, external, which prohibits foreign funding for political parties.

La législation signifie que les partis ne peuvent accepter de l’argent que d’un donateur autorisé. Les donateurs autorisés comprennent les personnes inscrites sur une liste électorale et les entreprises enregistrées au Royaume-Uni.

The legislation means parties can only accept money from a permissible donor. Permissible donors include individuals registered on an electoral register and companies registered in the UK.

It follows reports in the Guardian that some donations made to them and to Tice's firm Britain Means Business had been flagged to the agency as part of its Suspicious Activity Reports (SARs) programme.

Selon la NCA, ces alertes sont adressées aux forces de l'ordre "en cas de cas potentiels de blanchiment d'argent".

According to the NCA, such alerts are made to law enforcement "to potential instances of money laundering".
