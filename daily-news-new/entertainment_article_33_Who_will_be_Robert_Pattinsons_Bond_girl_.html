Mais il y a aussi la question plus large de savoir si l'idée de la fille Bond est maintenant quelque peu dépassée.

But then there’s the wider question of whether the whole idea of a “Bond girl” is now somewhat outdated.

Historiquement, le personnage avait peu d'agence et existait principalement comme intérêt romantique de Bond - et le terme "fille" lui-même est également critiqué pour infantiler les femmes.

Historically, the character had little agency and existed primarily as Bond's romantic interest - and the term "girl" itself is also criticised for infantilising women.

Monica Bellucci a rejeté le label. Elle a déclaré au Guardian, en dehors avant son apparition dans Spectre en 2015: "Je ne peux pas dire que je suis une Bond fille parce que je suis trop mature pour être une Bond fille. Je dis Bond dame; Bond femme".

Monica Bellucci rejected the label. She told the Guardian, external ahead of her appearance in Spectre in 2015: "I can't say I'm a Bond girl because I'm too mature to be a Bond girl. I say Bond lady; Bond woman."

The way the films have treated Bond girls "hasn't always aged well", says Rose.

Some scenes from 1960s Bond films are "basically rape", and "wouldn't fly today", said No Time To Die director Cary Fukunaga in 2021, external - referring to Thunderball and Goldfinger.

In reality, we have come a long way since then.

Over the years, producers have taken steps to usher the franchise into the modern era.

These days, the women of Bond are now much more likely to be sharp, capable equals to 007 - and what's defined as "attractive" has also evolved.

Belluci's inclusion in Spectre as a "Bond woman" aged 50 was widely praised for spotlighting a different type of sex appeal (even though she was only four years older than Daniel Craig in real life).

We've also had other powerful women in Bond, such as Michelle Yeoh's Colonel Wai Lin and Lashana Lynch's MI6 agent Nomi, who also made history as the first black female 007.

Rather than fitting the traditional Bond girl mould, both of them were competent women who could give Bond a run for his money.

"Nous avons dévié du rôle de quelqu'un qui a un nom stupide et que Bond leur tapotât le cul", dit le critique de cinéma Ivan-Zadeh. "Cela se déplace dans la bonne direction".

"We've moved away from the role being someone with a silly name and for Bond to pat on their bottom," says film critic Ivan-Zadeh. "It's moving in the right direction".

Another way the roles could evolve is by bringing in "a queer or sexually fluid Bond woman," suggests Ivan-Zadeh. "Someone whose desires don’t simply revolve around Bond. That would create some genuinely interesting chemistry.”

“For me, it's about giving these women proper storylines,” says Onafuye. "They should have their own motivations and their own ambitions.”

None of this is to say we should do away with the traditional Bond glamour.

"No-one wants to see Bond girls slumping around in their PJs," says Ivan-Zadeh.

But the important distinction is that "being sexy shouldn't be the only thing a female character has to offer,” says Onafuye.

"A ceux qui disent que les femmes de Bond sont bien de se sentir sexy et chaudes, je dirais qu'il n'y a rien de plus sexy ou de plus chaud qu'une femme (ou un homme) avec un cerveau et un esprit", ajoute la journaliste Caroline Frost.

“To those arguing that it's fine for Bond's women just to be sexy and hot, I'd say there's nothing sexier or hotter than a woman (or man) with a brain and a wit,” adds entertainment journalist Caroline Frost.

But continuing to evolve is crucial, says Chapman. "It's about retaining the humour and history of Bond but also recognising - this is 2026."

There are, of course, lots of other women within the films, and for Frost, the key is to have a range of different Bond women on screen, "all well matched with 007 in terms of strength, resources and, above all, wit".
